Why would anyone believe you?

Five habits to copy from these projects

1

Name the source. Let people check you.

2

Put a date on it. Data ages.

3

Show unknown as unknown. A gap is information. A guess is a risk.

4

Say what it cannot tell you. A pattern is not proof about one person.

5

Count everything, not one case. A full list is fairer than one scandal.

Division of labour

What the AI does, and what stays with you

Let the AI do

  • Read 300 pages and find the rule
  • Clean a messy table or scan
  • Write the code for a map
  • Draft, translate, simplify

You stay in charge of

  • Choosing a question that matters here
  • Checking every number at the source
  • Protecting names and privacy
  • Deciding what is fair to publish

Your one-page civic project card

Six boxes. Fifteen minutes.

1 · Problem

What goes wrong, where, and for whom?

2 · Question

One sentence, with a number in the answer.

3 · Data

Space, crowd, state or your phone: which one has it?

4 · Format

Map, checker, audit, game, simulation or explainer?

5 · User

Who opens it, on what phone, and what do they do next?

6 · Risk

What could be wrong or unfair, and how will you check?

Starter prompt · paste into any AI chatbot

Make the AI your project partner

Act as a civic data partner. My community problem is: [problem, place].

Help me turn it into one question with a number in the answer. Suggest three public data sources I could check, the best format (map, checker, audit, game or simulation), and what could mislead people.

Do not invent numbers. Tell me exactly what I must verify myself.

Then push back: ask it what is wrong with its own answer.
