## What the session covers

One curious person, public data and an AI assistant: what you can build for your community.
The live examples come from [mariuscomper.uk](https://mariuscomper.uk/en/), where Marius Comper
publishes maps, audits, tools and games built with AI tools from public records.

1. A one-minute warm-up game
2. A five-step recipe: sharp question, public data, AI as builder, something people use, honest limits
3. Seven live demos, each with a "Ghana remix" based on data that exists for Ghana
4. Five habits that make a civic project trustworthy
5. A 30-minute team exercise: design the Ghanaian version of one project

## Data that exists for Ghana

| Source | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| NASA night-time lights | Where the power goes out |
| Sentinel-2 satellite images | Galamsey, floods, new buildings |
| OpenStreetMap | Roads, clinics, schools, Accra's drains, trotro routes and stops |
| Breathe Accra / OpenAQ | Live air-quality sensors in Accra |
| Ghana Statistical Service, 2021 census | District-level data, for example households with no toilet |
| GHANEPS | Tenders and awarded public contracts |
| Auditor-General reports | Yearly audits of the district assemblies (PDF) |
| Your phone | Time it, count it, photograph it, date it |

Figures on the slides were checked against their sources on 20 November 2026. Check them again before reusing them.

## Starter prompt for the exercise

> Act as a civic data partner. My community problem is: [problem, place]. Help me turn it into one
> question with a number in the answer. Suggest three public data sources I could check, the best
> format (map, checker, audit, game or simulation), and what could mislead people. Do not invent
> numbers. Tell me exactly what I must verify myself.
